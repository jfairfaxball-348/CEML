## Theorem A — collapse of an authenticated finite branching quotient

Let T(x)=x/2 for positive even x and T(x)=(3x+1)/2 for positive odd x. An *edge certificate* (x,a;y,b) consists of two full authentic parity words, true positive orbits of respective lengths a,b, and a verified positive meeting value T^a(x)=T^b(y). Let G be any finite undirected graph of such edges, with arbitrary branching, cycles, repeated terminals and unrelated witness times. Assume a component V has finite minimum m.

1. **Composition:** if T^a(x)=T^b(y) and T^c(y)=T^d(z), put k=max(b,c). Then
   T^(a+k-b)(x)=T^k(y)=T^(d+k-c)(z).
   Applying this identity along a finite graph path produces a SINGLE authenticated meeting witness between every pair of vertices in V, including (x,m). Extra tail branches do not produce a stronger semantic certificate than a direct two-path witness.
2. **Exact skip criterion:** For a vertex n, this *finite component alone* licenses an induction-based skip precisely when it exposes some positive member m<n. The extracted pairwise meeting with that m proves that if m's trajectory reaches 1 then n's does. Strong induction (using the external A(B) below B, and induction on n for n>=B) is valid even when m is outside [B,E), provided 0<m<n. Equality m=n, or a proposed representative greater than n, yields no inductive reduction. A connected component with no strictly smaller authenticated member is not a skip certificate.
3. **No-cost amplification is impossible:** Graph minimisation can change which existing pairwise witness is selected, but cannot justify a new exclusion without (i) actually discovering a previously unaccepted strict lower pairwise witness, or (ii) proving and counting a uniform disjunction of such witnesses on disjoint integer classes. Quotient cardinality is not automatically a lower bound on integer exclusions. In particular, knowing that a sample in one residue cylinder has a small representative cannot be broadcast to the whole cylinder without family-specific parity, inequality, CRT and endpoint evidence.

**Proof.** Determinism of T gives the displayed identity by applying the appropriate nonnegative numbers of steps to each meeting. Induct along any finite path, so cycles or aliasing cannot invalidate a true certificate. Select numerical minimum m (the finite component assumption guarantees its existence). For n>m, use strong induction to establish convergence of m before transferring convergence along the meeting equality. When m=n there is no strictly smaller hypothesis; when m>n the induction hypothesis likewise does not apply. The composition also proves the claimed normal form: every valid graph-derived skip has a two-path witness. Conversely a pairwise certified smaller member can be regarded as a one-edge component. The last claim follows because a finite witness about one specific integer proves nothing about other integers not represented by that certificate. QED.
