# Road to 3 × 10²¹ — initial research/feasibility baseline

**Date:** 2026-10-14 (Europe/London). **Status:** research orientation, not a certified independent frontier, implementation approval, finance budget or scientific result. **Target:** every positive integer below the *exclusive* bound **3,000,000,000,000,000,000,000** justified by a contiguous chain of mathematically sound and independently defensible computational work. The Collatz conjecture itself remains unproved.

## Illustrative compute scale — NOT a CEML benchmark

The author's homepage advertises approximately **five seconds per 2^40-number work unit on a modern GPU**, inclusive of sieve exclusions. Applying this highly simplified uniform rate to 552,501,489 + 350,507,499,520/2^40 equivalent units gives about **2.762×10^9 GPU-seconds = 87.54 365.25-day GPU-years** of direct worker time. At 100% effective utilisation this corresponds to about 87.5 calendar years for 1 equivalent GPU, 8.75 years for 10, 320 days for 100 or 32 days for 1,000. These are *ideal illustrative compute equivalents*, not expected completion dates: the five-second figure is not demonstrated for prospective hardware, batching, independently verifying, overflow events, partial units, retries, load imbalance, cooling, work assignment, or electricity/budget constraints. Increasing the frontier could change costs and throughput. A 0.0001% fractional speedup saves about 46 GPU-minutes in this ideal model; target architectural gains and verified end-to-end benchmarks before micro-optimizing.

The 2025 paper states previous total usage **12,395 CPU-years and 159 GPU-years** across the then whole project, including old/slower workers, and thousands of parallel CPU/GPU workers on European HPC infrastructure. These are historical heterogeneous consumption measures, **not the wall-clock duration of one worker or the time needed for this new gap**. The first project record is 2019-09-04 and the 2^71 milestone 2025-01-15; concurrent workers and evolving algorithms mean elapsed historical duration is not proportional to volume. The paper reported up to ~52× GPU-vs-CPU and ~1,335× first-CPU-to-best-GPU speedups for its experimental configurations, not universally transferable factors.

## Four technical workstreams

### 1. Hardware and distribution
Compare laptop CPU feasibility as a research reference only; modern GPU single-node, HPC application, rented GPU fleet, volunteer network, existing Bařina client and independently administered cluster. Evaluate GPU architecture/driver, 128-bit arithmetic method, memory bandwidth and compressed sieve, per-worker throughput, scheduler complexity, failure recovery, queue fairness and checkpoint granularity. Match benchmark hardware and end-to-end workload. Network usually moves work-unit IDs and compact records, not full trajectories, but coordinator persistence, reputation, auditing, error recovery and node attestation still matter. At five seconds per work unit, 1,000 devices would demand roughly 200 completed work-unit reports/second; verify this scheduler rate rather than assuming negligible overhead.

### 2. Software and independent verification
Compare the accepted CEML GMP-focused CPU/ascending 9,999-integer batch-and-replay pipeline with the author's open source https://github.com/xbarin02/collatz and its https://github.com/xbarin02/collatz/blob/master/doc/ALGORITHM.md . His documented worker uses n+1 representation, trailing-zero counts (CTZ), grouped multiplications by powers of 3, modular sieves, C/C++-style CPU code and OpenCL GPU code with 128-bit fast arithmetic/overflow procedures. The paper tested compressed large 2^k masks and congruence-class concurrency. Preserve a **separate trusted reference** while benchmarking candidate optimized kernels. Do not silently replace existing CEML identity-bound worker or accept a speedup that drops coverage.

The main design question is separating production throughput from audit expense. Full independent per-integer replay of each 9,999-sized batch will not necessarily scale to hundreds of millions of 2^40-sized units. A checksum of intermediate branch counts is useful for detecting some errors but alone cannot publicly establish completeness or computation correctness; devise statistically meaningful duplicate-checking *and* an explicit trust model, or a stronger independently checkable mathematical certificate. Specify sample coverage and fraud/fault probabilities, exactly what is formally proved, what requires recomputation, and what claims the resulting evidence permits.

### 3. Proof-safe mathematics
The legitimate stopping theorem is conditional: if every strictly smaller positive integer has already been proved to converge (including an accepted prior frontier and all committed provisional starts), then any exact forward orbit descending below its own starting value inherits that result. Rule out even starts by n→n/2; odd n≡1 (mod 4) descent in two shortened-map steps; prove 3^k reverse-coalescence witnesses **from smaller certified starts**, not merely a statistical tendency. Use the P1 exact mod-144 ledger: E2=72, Q4=36, P3=12, M9=4, S2K4-03=5, and explicit=**15 of 144** for the accepted small-rule implementation. This density is a *count of explicit candidates*, not the time fraction nor the best known possible sieve. Study Barina's experimental CPU 2^34-bit and GPU 2^24-bit sieve choices, plus 3^2 for CPU / 3^1 for GPU and their hardware cost, before selecting a new approach. A larger sieve can cost more to construct/cache/access than it saves.

For every proposed new skip: exact map and domain, reduction invariant, algebraic witness/inequality, proof of universal coverage for every parameter, exceptions and independent residue/orbit reference checks. Proof of logarithmic-density-one results, probabilistic typical descent or many sampled examples NEVER licenses discarding any remaining specified input.

### 4. Time, costs and validity
Model usable GPU-hours rather than headline counts; queue access and allocations, hardware-capital/rental quotations, availability, power draw, PUE, electricity prices, data durability, reproducible software builds, long-term maintenance, independent verification time and redundant replicas. Provide cost formulas and break-even thresholds with source dates before any budget decision. Distinguish compute completion, audit completion, publication and recognition of a stronger globally contiguous result.

## Strategic choices and decisive blockers

- **Contribute to/collaborate with existing frontier project:** likely avoids rebuilding the scheduler and already optimized worker; depends on author acceptance, reporting and validation protocols. No outreach is authorised yet.
- **Independently reproduce meaningful complete units or demonstrate a better sieve:** smaller scientifically defensible first impact; may not move headline frontier alone.
- **Build an independently auditable optimized GPU/distributed verifier:** maximum control, but high engineering and validation cost and possible duplication as the world frontier advances. No specification or implementation authorised yet.
- **Stay research-only/no build:** appropriate if sourced compute/resource budgets or independent audit requirements prove prohibitive. This is an admissible positive result.

Decision gate: an adequately sourced, sensitivity-tested achievable cost/time/throughput model; demonstrably proof-safe sieve; defensible distributed fault and verification design; confirmation of independent importance/overlap; funding/hardware route; owner approval. There is no assumed obligation to spend money or implement production code.
